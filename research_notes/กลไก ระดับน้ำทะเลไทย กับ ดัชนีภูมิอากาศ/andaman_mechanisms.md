# Physical mechanisms of interannual (2–7 yr) sea level variability in the Andaman Sea / Thai Andaman coast (Ko Taphao Noi) and their links to ENSO (SOI, Niño3.4), IOD (DMI) and monsoon indices

Scope note: the Ko Taphao Noi (KTN) GLOSS station #42 / PSMSL #446 sits at 7.83°N, 98.43°E, Phuket ([BODC GLOSS station sheet](https://www.bodc.ac.uk/resources/inventories/gloss_handbook/stations/42/); [PSMSL station 446](https://psmsl.org/data/obtaining/stations/446.php)). It lies on the **eastern boundary of the Bay of Bengal / Andaman Sea**, i.e. *downstream* (in the coastal-Kelvin-wave sense) of the equator → Sumatra → Andaman Sea → Myanmar waveguide. Most of the dynamical literature studies the "eastern Bay of Bengal" boundary as a whole; Andaman-specific interannual sea level studies are few (see Gaps).

Evidence labels used below: **[ESTABLISHED]** = supported by several independent studies/models/observations; **[SINGLE-STUDY]** = one paper or one dataset; **[UNVERIFIED]** = seen only in a search-engine snippet whose primary source I could not open.

## Q1. How do equatorial Indian Ocean winds, equatorial Kelvin waves and the coastal Kelvin waveguide (Sumatra → Andaman → Myanmar) transmit IOD/ENSO signals to the Andaman coast?

### Takeaway
Interannual sea level on the eastern rim of the Bay of Bengal (which includes the Andaman Sea coast) is overwhelmingly **remotely forced by equatorial Indian Ocean zonal wind anomalies**: equatorial westerly (easterly) anomalies drive downwelling (upwelling) equatorial Kelvin waves that hit Sumatra, split poleward, and travel counter-clockwise as coastal Kelvin waves along Sumatra → Andaman/Myanmar → around the bay, raising (lowering) coastal sea level, while local bay winds and heat/freshwater fluxes contribute little on the eastern boundary. **[ESTABLISHED]**

### Cited Findings
- Han & Webster (2002) forced a 4½-layer reduced-gravity Indian Ocean model with NCEP–NCAR fields 1958–1998 and ran controlled experiments separating bay wind, equatorial wind, river discharge and heat fluxes + P–E. Result: "Sea level changes in the bay result largely from wind variability, with a typical amplitude of 10 cm and occasionally 10–25 cm at an interannual timescale. **Near the eastern and northern boundaries, sea level anomalies (SLAs) are predominantly caused by equatorial wind variability, which generates coastal Kelvin waves that propagate into the bay along the eastern boundary.**" Heat fluxes + P–E effect "generally small"; interannual river discharge influence "negligible." — Han, W. & Webster, P.J. (2002), *Forcing mechanisms of sea level interannual variability in the Bay of Bengal*, J. Phys. Oceanogr. 32, 216–239, doi:10.1175/1520-0485(2002)032<0216:FMOSLI>2.0.CO;2 — [PDF](https://atoc.colorado.edu/~whan/webpage/Publications/hanwebster-jpo02.pdf)
- Quantitatively for the **eastern-boundary box** (the closest analogue to KTN) over 41 years of monthly output: correlation between the full solution and the equatorial-wind-only solution = **0.95**, std-dev ratio = **0.95**; correlation with the bay-wind-only solution only **0.28** (std ratio 0.26). In the eastern bay "influence of the bay wind is negligible" and SLAs are produced "primarily by the equatorial wind in all seasons of a year"; bay-wind effect "generally smaller than 2 cm". Coastal interannual SLAs typically −15 to +10 cm. — [Han & Webster 2002](https://atoc.colorado.edu/~whan/webpage/Publications/hanwebster-jpo02.pdf)
- Han & Webster note their result agrees with Clarke & Liu (1994, JPO), "who suggested that interannual variability of sea level along at least 6000 km of the north Indian Ocean coastline from the equator to Bombay is generated by equatorial wind variability, and effect of the alongshore wind stress is negligible" (Han & Webster dispute only the western-boundary part). — [Han & Webster 2002](https://atoc.colorado.edu/~whan/webpage/Publications/hanwebster-jpo02.pdf) (secondary citation of Clarke, A.J. & Liu, X. 1994, *Interannual sea level in the northern and eastern Indian Ocean*, J. Phys. Oceanogr. 24, 1224–1235)
- Seasonal analogue of the same mechanism (illustrates the pathway): spring and fall equatorial westerlies / Wyrtki jets pile up water in the eastern basin; "This high sea level signal with an amplitude of 2–6 cm propagates into the eastern and northern bay as coastal Kelvin waves, and it also radiates westward from the eastern boundary into the bay interior as Rossby waves." — [Han & Webster 2002](https://atoc.colorado.edu/~whan/webpage/Publications/hanwebster-jpo02.pdf)
- Han & Webster also found that SLAs caused by equatorial wind at the equator and by bay wind along the northern/western boundaries "are significantly correlated, reflecting the anomalous wind pattern associated with the **dipole mode event**." — [Han & Webster 2002](https://atoc.colorado.edu/~whan/webpage/Publications/hanwebster-jpo02.pdf)
- Aparna et al. (2012), using a stratified Indian Ocean model for 1957–2008 that decomposes SLA into coastal alongshore-wind forcing, remote equatorial forcing (incl. reflected Rossby waves) and interior-bay wind forcing: during **IOD** events Bay of Bengal "SLAs are forced both from the equator and by direct wind forcing", whereas during **ENSO** events they are "primarily equatorially forced, with only a minor contribution from direct wind forcing." — Aparna, S.G., McCreary, J.P., Shankar, D. & Vinayachandran, P.N. (2012), *Signatures of Indian Ocean Dipole and El Niño–Southern Oscillation events in sea level variations in the Bay of Bengal*, J. Geophys. Res. 117, C10012, doi:[10.1029/2012JC008055](http://doi.org/10.1029/2012JC008055) — [IAS repository record](https://repository.ias.ac.in/135905)
- Rao et al. (2010) used altimeter SSH 1993–2006 (plus SODA and HYCOM) to show Kelvin waves "propagate along the equatorial wave guide until reaching Sumatra, where they follow the coastal waveguide counter-clockwise around the perimeter of the Bay of Bengal", with "large interannual variability caused primarily by variability in equatorial westerly winds during boreal fall." — Rao, R.R. et al. (2010), *Interannual variability of Kelvin wave propagation in the wave guides of the equatorial Indian Ocean, the coastal Bay of Bengal and the southeastern Arabian Sea during 1993–2006*, Deep-Sea Research I 57(1), doi:[10.1016/j.dsr.2009.10.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dsr.2009.10.008) (DOI verified to resolve to Elsevier PII S0967063709002003) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259872694_Interannual_variability_of_Kelvin_wave_propagation_in_the_wave_guides_of_the_equatorial_Indian_Ocean_the_coastal_Bay_of_Bengal_and_the_southeastern_Arabian_Sea_during_1993-2006); content summary via [search result snippet](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/30635)
- Suresh et al. (2018) (linear continuously stratified model) show the downstream consequence of the same waveguide: during positive IOD, "equatorial easterlies force **upwelling Kelvin waves that travel to WCI [west coast of India] through the Bay of Bengal coastal waveguide**. Part of this opposite signal also transits slowly through the Bay of Bengal interior as Rossby waves." This implies the eastern bay rim (incl. Andaman) is on the upwelling (low-sea-level) path during positive IOD. — Suresh, I., Vialard, J., Lengaigne, M., Izumo, T., Parvathi, V. & Muraleedharan, P.M. (2018), *Sea level interannual variability along the west coast of India*, Geophys. Res. Lett. 45(22), 12440–12448, doi:10.1029/2018GL080972 — [IRD record](https://www.documentation.ird.fr/hor/fdi:010074815)
- Andaman-specific evidence that equatorial Kelvin waves actually enter the Andaman Sea: equatorial Kelvin waves "intruding into the Andaman Sea significantly modulate thermocline structure and circulation", altering internal-tide energy by >30% (Yang et al. 2025, JGR Oceans, doi:10.1029/2025JC022798 — [Wiley](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1029/2025JC022798)); "Kelvin waves from the equatorial Indian Ocean modulate the nonlinear internal waves in the Andaman Sea", with Kelvin-wave strength/vertical structure "closely related to the IOD" ([Environ. Res. Lett. 2023, doi:10.1088/1748-9326/acf05d](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/acf05d)); DMI influences Andaman Sea circulation and density stratification on interannual scales ([Sci. Rep. 2022, *Interannual variability of internal tides in the Andaman Sea: an effect of Indian Ocean Dipole*, doi:10.1038/s41598-022-15301-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-15301-8)). [These three are summarised from search snippets; full texts were not accessible to me.]
- Mooring/altimetry evidence of remotely forced equatorial Kelvin and Rossby waves propagating into the Andaman Sea at intraseasonal (30–90 day) periods; equatorial forcing drives the mean coastal circulation around the Andaman–Nicobar islands — [*General circulation and principal wave modes in Andaman Sea from observations*, arXiv:2103.09771](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.09771) [snippet-level]

### Inferences
- Because coastal Kelvin waves are fast (first baroclinic mode ~2–3 m/s; from the Sumatra equator to Phuket is roughly 1,000–1,500 km along the coast), the equatorial signal should reach KTN within ~1 week — i.e. **effectively zero lag at monthly resolution** relative to the equatorial-wind-driven SLA off Sumatra. Any lag in a wavelet-coherence phase arrow will therefore reflect the lag between the *index* and the *equatorial wind*, not ocean propagation time. (Physical reasoning; the wave-speed figure is standard theory, not taken from a source fetched here.)
- KTN is on the Andaman (eastern) side of the Malay Peninsula and faces open water through the Andaman Sea; the Han & Webster "eastern boundary" result (r = 0.95 with equatorial forcing) is the most directly transferable quantitative result. One caveat: the Andaman Sea is partly separated from the bay by the Andaman–Nicobar ridge; coastal Kelvin waves follow the continental (Sumatra–Thailand–Myanmar) coast, so KTN should be *inside* the waveguide, but the channels between islands may leak part of the signal westward (inference).
- Since local wind / steric / river terms are small at the eastern boundary, coherence with SOI/Niño3.4/DMI at 2–7 yr is expected to be dynamically "real" and mediated mainly by equatorial zonal wind.

### Gaps
- No paper I could open quantifies the fraction of KTN interannual variance due to equatorial forcing vs Andaman-local wind; Han & Webster's eastern box is the Myanmar/Andaman sector broadly. A model decomposition specifically for Phuket was not found.
- Could not read the full texts of Rao et al. (2010), the 2022 Sci. Rep. or 2023 ERL Andaman papers (paywall / captcha), so their event-specific numbers are not recorded.

## Q2. Observed sign and magnitude of interannual SLA in the eastern Indian Ocean / Andaman Sea during positive IOD / El Niño and negative IOD / La Niña; Andaman-specific altimetry studies

### Takeaway
Positive IOD and El Niño years produce **negative (low) sea level** in the eastern equatorial Indian Ocean and along the eastern Bay of Bengal / Andaman coast; negative IOD / La Niña produce **positive (high)** SLA. At Phuket the 1997–98 positive IOD (with the 1997 El Niño) is the canonical example: ~7 months of depressed sea level (Sep 1997–Mar 1998), max depression ~29 cm, killing reef-flat corals by aerial exposure. **[ESTABLISHED for sign; magnitudes SINGLE-STUDY]**

### Cited Findings
- Model and TOPEX/Poseidon altimetry both show "a **positive SLA in the eastern tropical ocean during a negative dipole** (Nov 1996) and a **negative SLA in the same region during a positive dipole** (Nov 1997)"; during the negative dipole of 1998, "SLA is positive in the eastern tropical ocean in August." — [Han & Webster 2002](https://atoc.colorado.edu/~whan/webpage/Publications/hanwebster-jpo02.pdf)
- Bay of Bengal composites 1957–2008: during **positive IOD**, "negative SLAs are observed during April–December, with the SLAs decreasing continuously to a peak during **September–November**"; during **El Niño**, "negative SLAs are observed **twice (April–December and November–July)**, with a relaxation between the two peaks." — [Aparna et al. 2012, JGR 117, C10012](https://repository.ias.ac.in/135905)
- Phuket: "Mean sea level at Phuket has a marked seasonal component which results in a **20 cm** variation between the dry season (December–April) and the wet season (May–November). In addition, there may be larger variations of up to **±46 cm** (including seasonal variation) which are linked to Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) phenomena. **Positive IOD events lead to depressed sea levels around Phuket** ... One of the most pronounced events took place in **1997–98** when sea levels were depressed over a 7 month period from **September 1997–March 1998** with a maximum sea-level depression of **29 cm**." — Brown, B.E., Dunne, R.P., Somerfield, P.J., Edwards, A.J., Simons, W.J.F., Phongsuwan, N., Putchim, L., Anderson, L. & Naeije, M.C. (2019), *Long-term impacts of rising sea temperature and sea level on shallow water coral communities over a ~40 year period*, Scientific Reports 9, 8273, doi:[10.1038/s41598-019-45188-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-45188-x) — [PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/149721/1/s41598-019-45188-x.pdf)
- Altimetry near Phuket (1993–mid-2016, U. Colorado "sea level wizard"): anomalies "largely positive" at the start of the series, then the large 1997–98 depression; "no marked negative anomalies until the **positive IOD of 2007–2008** when sea levels, although lowered, were not as severely depressed as in 1997–98. Post-2008, sea-level anomalies appear to have been generally positive until the end of the time series in mid-2016." Brown et al. also describe "the marked negative IOD sea-level anomaly in 1998" (they mean a negative *sea-level* anomaly caused by the 1997 positive IOD) and "in 2007 a smaller but nonetheless sizeable negative anomaly". — [Brown et al. 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/149721/1/s41598-019-45188-x.pdf)
- The original Phuket analysis (tide gauge + reef data) is Brown, B.E., Dunne, R.P., Phongsuwan, N. & Somerfield, P.J. (2011), *Increased sea level promotes coral cover on shallow reef flats in the Andaman Sea, eastern Indian Ocean*, Coral Reefs 30, 867–878, doi:10.1007/s00338-011-0804-9 — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/226366082_Increased_sea_level_promotes_coral_cover_on_shallow_reef_flats_in_the_Andaman_Sea_eastern_Indian_Ocean). Search snippet of this paper: "The exceptional lowering of sea levels in the Andaman Sea was due to the pronounced Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) of 1997" and "for the period March 1998 to December 2009, the IOD DMI and mean sea level are correlated." [snippet only; correlation value and sign not seen]
- Earlier coral work also attributes the 1997–98 Phuket reef-flat mortality to "anomalously low sea levels in 1997–1998 as a result of climate-related events in the Indian Ocean." — Brown, B.E., Clarke, K.R. & Warwick, R.M. (2002), Mar. Biol. 141, 21–29, doi:[10.1007/s00227-002-0810-0](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00227-002-0810-0)
- 2019 extreme positive IOD: "The eastern equatorial Indian Ocean featured cold SST anomalies, **lower sea level**, and shallow thermocline to the south of the equator" (Climate Dynamics 2025, doi:10.1007/s00382-025-07914-2 — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00382-025-07914-2)); upwelling off Nusa Tenggara in 2019 had SLA ≈ **−15 cm** ([Indonesian journal article via BRIN](https://karya.brin.go.id/11563/1/Jurnal_Guntur%20Adhi%20R_KKP_2019.pdf)) [both snippet-level; these are south of the equator, not the Andaman].

### Inferences
- **Sign expectations for KTN (key result for the WTC phase interpretation):**
  - DMI positive → KTN sea level low → **anti-phase** (≈180°) with DMI.
  - Niño3.4 positive (El Niño) → KTN low → **anti-phase** with Niño3.4.
  - SOI negative during El Niño → KTN low → **in-phase** (≈0°) with SOI.
  - Negative IOD / La Niña (e.g. 1996, 1998, 2010–11, 2016) → expected high KTN sea level (sign supported by the 1996/1998 TOPEX maps in Han & Webster and the "generally positive" post-2008 anomalies in Brown et al. 2019; I did not find a source specifically quantifying 2010–11 at Phuket).
- **Lag expectations:** for IOD, SLA peaks with the IOD itself (Sep–Nov), so ≈0 lag relative to DMI. For ENSO, the Aparna et al. "double dip" (Apr–Dec of the developing year, then Nov–Jul across the Niño3.4 peak into the following year) implies KTN SLA lows co-occur with or slightly *lag* the Niño3.4 peak (boreal winter) by 0 to ~6 months for the second dip. In a wavelet-coherence plot, arrows somewhat off pure anti-phase (e.g. sea level lagging Niño3.4 by a few months) would be physically consistent.
- 1994, 2006, 2015, 2019 positive IODs: expected low KTN sea level by the same mechanism; 2006/2015 magnitudes at Phuket not documented in sources I could open (Brown et al. 2019 only flagged 1997–98 and 2007–08 as marked within 1993–2016 — interesting, since 2006 was a notable positive IOD; the 2007–08 anomaly they cite may partly be the tail of 2006 plus the 2007/2008 positive IODs).

### Gaps
- No altimetry study *focused on* Andaman Sea interannual SLA with event-by-event magnitudes (1994, 2006, 2015, 2019, 2010–11) was found and opened.
- The Brown et al. (2011) correlation value between DMI and KTN MSL (Mar 1998–Dec 2009) could not be retrieved.
- Search snippets (from the 2022 Sci. Rep. / related Andaman results) contained the claim that "in the near-equatorial Andaman Sea area, sea level variability is more synchronized with ENSO rather than IOD", while "the IOD, via coastal Kelvin wave propagation, induces interannual sea-level variability in the northern part of the Strait of Malacca." **[UNVERIFIED]** — I could not identify which paper said this (possibly Sangmanee et al. 2026, JGR Oceans, *Interannual variability of along-strait flow in the Malacca Strait and its dynamics*, doi:10.1029/2026JC024202, [Wiley](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2026JC024202?af=R)). Treat as a lead to check, not a finding.

## Q3. How do ENSO and IOD co-vary, and why would KTN sea level be coherent with both SOI/Niño3.4 and DMI at the same periods?

### Takeaway
Positive IODs frequently co-occur with El Niño (and negative IODs with La Niña) because El Niño shifts the Walker circulation's descending branch over the Maritime Continent/eastern Indian Ocean, producing equatorial **easterly** anomalies off Sumatra–Java — exactly the wind anomaly that also drives upwelling Kelvin waves and low sea level at the eastern boundary. So DMI and Niño3.4/SOI share a common 2–7 yr band and a common wind pathway to KTN; but a sizeable fraction of IODs are independent of ENSO, which is why the two indices are not redundant. **[ESTABLISHED]**

### Cited Findings
- "Positive IOD events are typically accompanied by El Niño events, while negative IOD events are commonly linked to La Niña events. However, many IOD events are shown to occur independently of the El Niño"; "the major fraction of the IOD Mode Index is due to regional processes within the Indian Ocean." — Ashok, K., Guan, Z. & Yamagata, T. (2003), *A look at the relationship between the ENSO and the Indian Ocean Dipole*, J. Meteorol. Soc. Japan 81(1), 41–56 — [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmsj/81/1/81_1_41/_article); [record](https://dspace.uohyd.ac.in/handle/1/2613) [content from search summary]
- "The ENSO-induced IOD events are forced by a zonal shift in the descending branch of the Walker circulation over the eastern Indian Ocean"; canonical El Niño weakens the Indo-Pacific Walker circulation, reducing rainfall over the eastern tropical Indian Ocean/Maritime Continent and producing "surface easterly wind anomalies off Java–Sumatra, which under the Bjerknes feedback induce cold SST anomalies." — search summary of [Wang & Wang 2014 (NOAA AOML)](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/phod/docs/X.Wang_C.%20Wang_2014.pdf) and related [Xiao et al. 2022, Sci. Rep.](https://www.ess.uci.edu/~yu/PDF/Xiao%20et%20al.Scientific%20Reports.2022.pdf) [attribution of each sentence to a specific one of these two papers not verified]
- IOD state can feed back on ENSO: "negative IODs tending to precede the development of El Niño events" — Izumo, T. et al. (2010), *Influence of the state of the Indian Ocean Dipole on the following year's El Niño*, Nature Geoscience 3, 168–172 — [PDF](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Izumo_etal_NG2010.pdf) [search summary]
- The model of Aparna et al. (2012) finds the *process* differs: ENSO-related Bay of Bengal SLAs are "primarily equatorially forced", IOD-related SLAs are forced both equatorially and by direct (bay) wind — [Aparna et al. 2012](https://repository.ias.ac.in/135905)
- Han & Webster model reproduces the 1958–1998 dipole history (incl. negative 1996, positive 1997, moderate negative 1998) and ties eastern-boundary SLAs to dipole-related equatorial winds — [Han & Webster 2002](https://atoc.colorado.edu/~whan/webpage/Publications/hanwebster-jpo02.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a wavelet-coherence study: KTN can show significant coherence with Niño3.4/SOI **and** with DMI in the same 2–7 yr band without implying two independent mechanisms — the shared mediator is equatorial Indian Ocean zonal wind. Partial wavelet coherence (e.g. KTN vs DMI controlling for Niño3.4) is the natural test of whether DMI adds independent information.
- Expect the strongest coherence in epochs with large co-occurring events (1994*, 1997–98, 2006, 2015, 2019; *1994 was a positive IOD with a weak/modest El Niño), and weaker ENSO coherence when IOD is "pure" or when ENSO is not accompanied by equatorial Indian Ocean easterlies.
- Phase difference between DMI and Niño3.4 themselves (DMI peaks Sep–Nov, Niño3.4 peaks Nov–Jan) means KTN–Niño3.4 phase arrows may show KTN *leading* Niño3.4 slightly if the IOD-season dip dominates, or *lagging* if the post-peak (Nov–Jul) dip dominates (combines Aparna et al.'s timing with standard index seasonality — inference).

### Gaps
- No source fetched gave a quantitative IOD–ENSO co-occurrence fraction for 1950–present; recent reviews (e.g. CLIVAR/IndOOS-2 decadal review, Stuecker et al. 2017 ENSO–IOD "combination mode") were not opened in this pass.

## Q4. Indonesian Throughflow / Pacific signals via the Indonesian seas — relevance to the Andaman Sea?

### Takeaway
Pacific (ENSO) sea level signals do leak into the Indian Ocean via the Indonesian seas, but the waveguide geometry sends them **south/east** (Nusa Tenggara → NW Australia → west Australian coast), not northward to the Andaman Sea. Meanwhile, the Sumatra–Java–Nusa Tenggara coast is where Indian Ocean equatorial signals turn **south**, while the Sumatra–Andaman branch carries them **north**. The Pacific → ITF route is therefore likely a minor, indirect contributor at KTN; the ENSO signal at KTN arrives mainly via the atmosphere (Walker circulation → equatorial Indian Ocean winds). **[ESTABLISHED for geometry; relevance-to-Andaman is inference]**

### Cited Findings
- Wijffels & Meyers (2004): XBT data since 1983 show that "equatorial Indian Ocean wind energy propagates along the Sumatra–Java–Nusa Tenggara waveguide to penetrate the Savu Sea, the western Banda Sea and Makassar Strait"; the region "comprises the **intersection of two ocean waveguides**" (Indian Ocean equatorial/coastal and Pacific). — Wijffels, S. & Meyers, G. (2004), *An intersection of oceanic waveguides: variability in the Indonesian Throughflow region*, J. Phys. Oceanogr. 34(5), 1232–1253 — [eCite UTAS](https://ecite.utas.edu.au/46019) [summary from search]
- Interannual sea level in the eastern Indian and western Pacific oceans is "dominated by ENSO and modulated by PDO" — search summary associated with a Climate Dynamics study of the SCS/eastern Indian Ocean, doi:10.1007/s00382-015-2756-1 [UNVERIFIED attribution; I could not open the paper, and the snippet mixes SCS-focused findings].

### Inferences
- The Andaman Sea is "upstream" of the Indonesian-seas intersection in the Indian Ocean coastal Kelvin-wave sense (waves on the Sumatra coast propagate with the coast on their right in the NH, i.e. northward toward Andaman; south of the equator they go southeastward toward Java). Pacific signals entering via Lombok/Ombai/Timor would propagate poleward along the *Australian* coast. Hence a direct ITF/Pacific oceanic pathway into the Andaman Sea is not expected; an ENSO signature at KTN should be attributed to atmospheric teleconnection onto Indian Ocean winds.
- The Malacca Strait connects the Andaman Sea to the South China Sea; a ±few-cm ENSO signal on the Malaysian side (see Q6 snippet) could reach the southern Andaman Sea through the strait, but this is shallow/frictional and likely weak at Phuket (speculative).

### Gaps
- Did not find a study explicitly testing whether Pacific-origin signals reach the Andaman Sea through the Malacca Strait or Indonesian seas.

## Q5. Local effects: monsoon wind setup, steric (thermal expansion), river discharge (Irrawaddy/Salween)

### Takeaway
On the eastern Bay of Bengal boundary, model decompositions find **local (bay) wind, heat-flux/P–E (steric), and interannual river discharge contributions are small** compared with equatorial remote forcing at interannual scales; local monsoon forcing dominates the large *seasonal* cycle (~20 cm at Phuket) but not the interannual anomalies. **[ESTABLISHED for eastern boundary of the bay as a whole; Andaman-specific SINGLE-STUDY/none]**

### Cited Findings
- Eastern-bay box: bay-wind-forced SLA has r = 0.28 with total, std ratio 0.26, "generally smaller than 2 cm"; "the effect of heat fluxes + P–E is generally small"; "influence of interannual variability of river discharges is negligible"; anomalous precipitation "can modify the sea level by as much as 1 cm." Seasonal river discharge raises sea level by 2–6 cm in some bay regions (May–July). — [Han & Webster 2002](https://atoc.colorado.edu/~whan/webpage/Publications/hanwebster-jpo02.pdf) (note: their river forcing covers bay rivers; whether Irrawaddy/Salween are individually resolved was not checked)
- Phuket seasonal cycle ≈ 20 cm between dry (Dec–Apr) and wet (May–Nov) seasons — [Brown et al. 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/149721/1/s41598-019-45188-x.pdf)
- ENSO modulates Bay of Bengal summer monsoon onset (delayed after a warm ENSO), relevant to Andaman local winds/rain — Li, K. et al., GRL (cited in [Brown et al. 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/149721/1/s41598-019-45188-x.pdf), ref. 39)
- For ENSO-related Bay of Bengal SLA, "only a minor contribution from direct wind forcing" — [Aparna et al. 2012](https://repository.ias.ac.in/135905)

### Inferences
- Coherence of KTN with **Asian / western-North-Pacific monsoon wind indices** (e.g. WNPMI, Webster–Yang, Indian monsoon indices) at 2–7 yr is most plausibly *indirect*: these indices are themselves strongly ENSO-modulated (and the Indian-monsoon circulation interacts with IOD), so they inherit ENSO/IOD timing. Direct Andaman-local monsoon wind setup should matter less on interannual scales given the Han & Webster eastern-box numbers. A partial-coherence test controlling for Niño3.4/DMI would discriminate.
- Inverse-barometer effects (SLP anomalies, ~1 cm per hPa) are a possible small local term not discussed by the sources found; ENSO-related SLP anomalies over the Maritime Continent are of order 1 hPa, so ≲1–2 cm (physical reasoning, not sourced).

### Gaps
- No study found quantifying Irrawaddy/Salween interannual discharge effects on Andaman (eastern side) sea level specifically.
- No study found that directly relates KTN to WNP/Asian monsoon wind indices.

## Q6. Studies of Thai Andaman tide gauges (Ko Taphao Noi, Phuket, Ranong, Satun) relating sea level to ENSO/IOD — plus data caveats

### Takeaway
The main published link between a Thai Andaman gauge and IOD is the Brown et al. coral-reef series (2011 Coral Reefs; 2019 Sci. Rep.), which attributes the 1997–98 depression to the positive IOD and reports a DMI–MSL correlation for 1998–2009 at KTN. Crucially for any time-series analysis, the KTN record is affected by **2004 Sumatra–Andaman earthquake vertical land motion** and by **datum problems after a 2013–15 refurbishment**. ### Inferences
- For a 2–7 yr wavelet coherence analysis of KTN: the post-2004 exponential-decay subsidence is a **low-frequency non-linear trend**; linear detrending will leave a residual bump that can leak into the long-period end (≳5–7 yr) of the band. Options: analyse pre-2004 and post-2004 separately, remove a GNSS-based VLM model, or use a higher-order/LOESS detrend; and inspect 2013–2015 for datum jumps (Brown et al. explicitly distrust this part).
- The large Phuket interannual range (up to ±46 cm incl. seasonal; 29 cm in 1997–98) vs. Han & Webster's typical eastern-boundary −15/+10 cm suggests KTN is a high-signal site for IOD/ENSO, which favours detectable coherence.
