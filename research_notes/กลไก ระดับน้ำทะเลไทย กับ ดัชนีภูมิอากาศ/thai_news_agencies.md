# Thai news and Thai/regional agency reports on unusual sea levels linked to ENSO, IOD and the monsoon (1990–2026)

Scope note: this covers Thai news, Thai agency pages, and one peer-reviewed long-term Phuket study that Thai agencies draw on. Pages were fetched on 2026-10-07. Where I quote a page, the quote is the Thai text that the fetch tool returned. Where only the fetch tool's English summary was available, I say so. Thai Buddhist-era years are converted to CE (BE − 543).

---

## Q1. What do Thai agencies (TMD, HII/thaiwater, the Navy Hydrographic Department, DMCR, PRD) report about sea level during El Niño, La Niña and IOD years?

### Takeaway
The clearest agency material comes from DMCR on the **Andaman** coast. It lists abnormally low sea levels in **1997, 2006 and 2019**, which are all positive-IOD years: about 30 cm below normal in 1997 and about 15 cm below normal in 2019. These low stands lasted 2–3 months and killed shallow corals. For the **Gulf of Thailand / Bangkok**, agencies describe high water ("น้ำทะเลหนุนสูง") as a recurring **seasonal, end-of-year** event. They attribute it to spring tides plus northeast-monsoon onshore wind and river runoff, not to ENSO. Even in the La Niña flood year of 2011, HII linked La Niña to *rainfall*, not to Gulf sea level.

### Cited Findings
**Andaman, low sea level in positive-IOD years (DMCR)**
- DMCR library article "ผลกระทบจากปรากฏการณ์น้ำลงมากผิดปกติต่อแนวปะการังทางฝั่งทะเลอันดามัน" ("Impact of abnormally low water on Andaman coral reefs"). Author: Niphon Phongsuwan (นิพนธ์ พงศ์สุวรรณ), Marine and Coastal Resources Research and Development Institute, January 2021. Its points:
  - Abnormally low water occurred in **1997 (2540), 2006 (2549) and 2019 (2562)**.
  - Water was "ต่ำกว่าปกติประมาณ 30 เซนติเมตร" (about 30 cm below normal) in 1997 and "ต่ำกว่าเดิมได้มากถึง 15 เซนติเมตร" (up to 15 cm lower) in 2019.
  - Shallow corals were "โผล่พ้นน้ำนานถึง 3–4 ชั่วโมง" (exposed for 3–4 hours) instead of the usual 2 hours, for "2-3 เดือน หรือ นานกว่านั้น" (2–3 months or longer).
  - Result: "ปะการังตรงโซนที่ตื้นตายไปเป็นจำนวนมาก" (many shallow-zone corals died).
  - The fetched page text did **not** name a climate mechanism (IOD/ENSO) or exact sites. — [DMCR detailLib/5320](https://www.dmcr.go.th/detailLib/5320)
  - Note: 1997, 2006 and 2019 match the positive-IOD years in our project list exactly.
- DMCR's Upper Andaman Sea Research Center, as quoted by Thai PBS on 8 Apr 2022, on Ko He (เกาะเฮ), Thalang, Phuket:
  - "พบว่าข้อมูลปี 62 ระดับน้ำทะเลลดลงอย่างผิดปกติ ประมาณ 15 ซม.และอยู่นาน" (2019 data show sea level abnormally lowered about 15 cm, and it lasted).
  - Less than 5% of the staghorn coral (*Acropora*) remained healthy.
  - People quoted: Kongkiat Kittiwatanawong (ก้องเกียรติ กิตติวัฒนาวงศ์, center director) and Niphon Phongsuwan.
  - The fetched article frames it as a natural phenomenon and does not name IOD. — [Thai PBS, 8 Apr 2022](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/314388)
- The same Ko He event, as reported by MGR Online on 4 Mar 2022:
  - Low water happened "ในช่วงปลายปี 2562" (late 2019).
  - About 1,000 m² of *Acropora* (ปะการังเขากวาง) died.
  - According to the fetch tool's summary, Niphon Phongsuwan attributed the low water to the **Indian Ocean Dipole**. I could not confirm a verbatim Thai quote naming IOD; check the page before quoting it. — [MGR Online, 4 Mar 2022](https://mgronline.com/south/detail/9650000021733)

**Andaman, IOD-driven cold water (TMD / national park, positive IOD)**
- Thai PBS, 3 Dec 2020, "เตือน 'IOD' รุกคืบทะเลสิมิลันน้ำเย็นเฉียบ 23 องศาฯ":
  - Similan Islands National Park reported cold intrusions down to **23 °C** and linked them to IOD, citing TMD.
  - The explanation given is that the thermocline shoals: "หากชั้นอุณหภูมิเข้าใกล้ 50 เมตร อาจมีน้ำเย็นเฉียบจากใต้มหาสมุทร" (if the thermocline comes within 50 m, very cold deep water can intrude).
  - This is a temperature report, not a sea-level report. It is mechanistically consistent with a raised thermocline and a lowered sea surface in the eastern Indian Ocean during a positive IOD. — [Thai PBS, 3 Dec 2020](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/287576)

**Gulf of Thailand, 2011 La Niña floods (HII/thaiwater)**
- HII's summary of the 2011 Great Flood:
  - "The La Niña phenomenon caused Thailand to have an increased rainfall trend… La Niña began in mid-2010 and continued until mid-2011 with strong intensity in late 2010," and returned to weak/moderate La Niña from Aug 2011 to Mar 2012.
  - Annual rainfall was 1,826 mm, 25% above normal.
  - High tides in the Gulf blocked drainage. The highest level was **2.53 m on 30 Oct 2011** and 2.41 m on 12 Nov 2011, at the Royal Thai Navy Hydrographic Department station at the Chao Phraya mouth.
  - HII gives **no mechanism** for the high tides and does not attribute them to La Niña. — [HII/thaiwater, 2011 flood summary](https://www.thaiwater.net/uploads/contents/current/2011/flood54Eng.html)

**Gulf of Thailand, seasonal high water (PRD, the Navy Hydrographic Department)**
- Government Public Relations Department, 3 Dec 2025, "เตรียมรับมือ 'น้ำทะเลหนุนสูง' ช่วงปลายปี":
  - High water recurs "ช่วงปลายปีของทุกปี" (late in every year).
  - Causes given:
    - lunar spring tides: "อิทธิพลของปรากฏการณ์น้ำขึ้นตามปฏิทินจันทรคติ"
    - northeast monsoon onshore wind: "ลมมรสุมตะวันออกเฉียงเหนือพัดน้ำเข้าฝั่ง"
    - dam releases and upstream runoff
  - It stresses: "น้ำทะเลหนุน ไม่ใช่น้ำท่วมจากฝน แต่เป็นปรากฏการณ์ธรรมชาติ" (high water is not rain flooding but a natural phenomenon). — [PRD, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/31/iid/450140)
- Samut Prakan provincial warning for July 2023:
  - At Fort Phra Chulachomklao station (ป้อมพระจุลจอมเกล้า), Navy Hydrographic Dept, high water was forecast at **≥1.30 m above MSL**.
  - This shows high-water warnings also come in the southwest-monsoon season. — [PRD, Samut Prakan July 2023](https://www.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/9/iid/192106)
- Other recent PRD/InfoQuest warnings:
  - 29 Sep – 4 Oct 2026: [PRD](https://www.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/33/iid/545961)
  - 7–13 Sep 2026, 6 central provinces + Bangkok: [InfoQuest](https://www.infoquest.co.th/2026/643775)

**TMD**
- TMD has El Niño / La Niña explainer pages, e.g. [tmd.go.th/climate/El-Nino-La-Nina](https://www.tmd.go.th/climate/El-Nino-La-Nina). The search snippets frame ENSO's effect on Thailand as a **rainfall** effect (La Niña wetter, El Niño drier). I did not find a TMD page linking ENSO to sea level on Thai coasts.

### Inferences
- Agency material splits the two coasts the same way our statistics do:
  - **Andaman:** sea-level anomalies are interannual and happen in specific climate-mode years. 1997, 2006 and 2019 are all positive-IOD years, and 1997 and 2019 also overlap El Niño.
  - **Gulf:** "น้ำทะเลหนุนสูง" is a seasonal calendar event that agencies explain with tide plus monsoon wind plus runoff, with no ENSO term. This matches our finding that Gulf high water is mostly seasonal/monsoonal and not coherent with ENSO at 2–7 years.
- In 2011 La Niña acted on Bangkok mainly through rainfall and runoff. The record 30 Oct 2011 tide fell in the usual Oct–Dec seasonal high-water window, so it cannot be cleanly attributed to La Niña from these sources.

### Gaps
- I found no TMD, the Navy Hydrographic Department, GISTDA or ONEP page that explicitly quantifies sea-level anomalies by ENSO or IOD phase. The TMD and ONEP climate reports were not fetched.
- I found no Thai agency report on Andaman or Gulf sea level during El Niño 2015/16 or 2023/24, or during the negative IOD of 2016.
- I could not verify a verbatim Thai quote from DMCR naming "IOD" as the cause of the 2019 low water; only the MGR summary says so.

## Q2. What did Thai news report about unusually high or low sea levels and exposed reefs, and how were they explained?

### Takeaway
The Thai press shows two different kinds of events:
- **Gulf of Thailand (Ko Thalu, Prachuap Khiri Khan):** corals exposed at low tide almost every year in June–August, which DMCR and scientists explicitly call **seasonal/tidal and harmless**.
- **Andaman (Phuket):** a 2019 low stand that lasted months and caused **coral mortality**.

Gulf *high* water in Bangkok/Samut Prakan is reported as an annual Oct–Dec event. The September 2026 "+80 cm above predicted" anomaly was attributed to a low-pressure system and storm surge, explicitly as a provisional explanation.

### Cited Findings
**Gulf, Ko Thalu low-tide coral exposure (seasonal)**
- Kapook, 18 Jun 2018, "ยันไม่ใช่สึนามิ !! หลังน้ำทะเลเกาะทะลุลงต่ำสุดในรอบ 20 ปี จนปะการังโผล่เหนือน้ำ":
  - Ko Thalu, Bang Saphan Noi, Prachuap Khiri Khan. The fetch summary garbled the island name; the headline says เกาะทะลุ.
  - Water was described as the "lowest in 20 years", receding about 70 cm and exposing reef for hundreds of metres.
  - Thon Thamrongnawasawat (ธรณ์ ธำรงนาวาสวัสดิ์, Kasetsart Fisheries) denied it was a tsunami. The event is framed as a tidal/seasonal low.
  - The foundation secretary quoted said the low lasted roughly 09:00–13:00 over three days, longer than in earlier years.
  - The "lowest in 20 years" claim is anecdotal, not instrumental. — [Kapook, 18 Jun 2018](https://travel.kapook.com/view195062.html)
- DMCR news, 19 Jun 2022: coral exposure at Ko Thalu on 16–18 Jun 2022.
  - It was the year's first lowest tide per the Navy Hydrographic Department tide tables.
  - DMCR said it was "ถือเป็นเรื่องธรรมชาติ" (a natural matter) with no harm to the reef. — [DMCR news 59762](https://www.dmcr.go.th/detailAll/59762/nws/)
  - The fetch summary mislabelled the province as Trang; the page text says Prachuap Khiri Khan.
- Thai PBS and MGR also reported Ko Thalu exposure in later years:
  - [Thai PBS 293382](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/293382)
  - [MGR 9670000053691](https://mgronline.com/local/detail/9670000053691)
  - [MGR 9650000066793, "lowest in 2 years"](https://mgronline.com/local/detail/9650000066793)
  - The search summary of these says such exposure "เกิดขึ้นเป็นประจำทุกปี… ในห้วงเดือนกรกฎาคมถึงสิงหาคม" (happens every year, July–August). I did not fetch these pages individually.

**Andaman, Phuket 2019 low sea level and coral death (interannual)**
- See the Q1 entries for Thai PBS (8 Apr 2022, −15 cm, <5% coral healthy) and MGR (4 Mar 2022, late 2019, IOD attribution per summary). — [Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/314388); [MGR](https://mgronline.com/south/detail/9650000021733)

**Gulf, Bangkok/Samut Prakan high water**
- The Active (Thai PBS), 7 Dec 2025, flooding in Samut Prakan, Nonthaburi, Samut Songkhram, Samut Sakhon and Chachoengsao:
  - The Navy Hydrographic Department warned of high water from 4–12 Dec 2025.
  - Chao Phraya levels were forecast at "ประมาณ 1.70 – 2.00 เมตร จากระดับทะเลปานกลาง" (about 1.70–2.00 m above MSL).
  - The cause given is only "น้ำทะเลหนุนสูง" (tidal high water); no climate mode is mentioned. — [The Active / Thai PBS, 7 Dec 2025](https://theactive.thaipbs.or.th/news/disaster-20051207)
  - Thai PBS on X, 6 Dec 2025, reported 60–80 cm of flooding in Phra Pradaeng and Phra Samut Chedi. — [Thai PBS on X](https://x.com/ThaiPBSNews/status/1997257654545297669)
  - Thairath reported vendors there say "เจอปัญหาทุกปี" (face it every year). — [Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/news/local/central/2900199)
- Bangkok Biz News, 7 Oct 2026 (sustainability section):
  - Inner-Gulf sea level rose **more than 80 cm above the predicted level** during **22–25 Sep 2026**, with heavy rain over Bangkok. The rise acted as a "water barrier" to drainage.
  - The data came from a station run with Kasetsart Fisheries and HII (สสน.).
  - Thon Thamrongnawasawat: "หย่อมความกดอากาศต่ำเหนือกรุงเทพมหานคร มีผลโดยตรงที่ทำให้ระดับน้ำทะเลปั่นป่วนและยกตัวสูงขึ้น" (the low over Bangkok directly disturbed and raised sea level), i.e. a storm-surge or inverse-barometer explanation.
  - He frames the link to the flooding as **preliminary or likely, not certain**, so treat it as a speculative explanation. — [Bangkok Biz News, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/sustainability/environment/1253941)

### Inferences
- These reports suit the paper as illustrations:
  - Ko Thalu is a **Gulf seasonal low-water** example: annual, Jun–Aug, described as harmless.
  - Phuket 2019 is an **Andaman interannual low-water** example: a positive-IOD/El Niño year, a multi-month anomaly, and mortality.
  - Bangkok Oct–Dec high water is a **Gulf seasonal high-water** example.
- Journalists' explanations of Gulf events point to tide, monsoon and weather systems, never to ENSO. Andaman explanations, when they give one, point to IOD.

### Gaps
- I did not find contemporaneous (1997/98) Thai newspaper reports of the Andaman low stand. The 1997 evidence comes from DMCR retrospectives and the peer-reviewed Phuket series below.
- I found no Bangkok Post or The Nation English article explicitly tying an Andaman sea-level event to IOD. The English search mostly returned scientific papers.
- I found no news of an Andaman *high* sea-level event (e.g., during the 2016 negative IOD or 2010–2012 La Niña).
- BBC Thai, Matichon and Prachachat were not searched specifically.

## Q4. Thai or Thailand-based scientific explanations of the mechanism

### Takeaway
The strongest mechanism statement comes from a 34-year Phuket reef study co-authored with DMCR scientists (Dunne, Brown, Phongsuwan, Putchim 2021). It finds that ENSO and IOD "modulate sea level and temperature" in the Andaman Sea. It identifies **combined positive IOD + El Niño sea-level depression** as the main cause of the 1997 coral loss, and **pIOD + El Niño lowered sea level plus heat** in 2019. This is direct, peer-reviewed support for our Andaman–ENSO/IOD coherence result.

### Cited Findings
- Dunne R.P., Brown B.E., Phongsuwan N., Putchim L. (2021). "The Indian Ocean Dipole and El Niño Southern Oscillation as major drivers of coral cover on shallow reefs in the Andaman Sea." *Global Change Biology* 27(14): 3312–3323. doi:10.1111/gcb.15640. Abstract quotes:
  - "fluctuating decadal/intradecadal climate processes of El Niño Southern Oscillation (ENSO) and Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD), which modulate sea level and temperature, are the main drivers of coral cover"
  - "the three major reductions in cover in 1997, 2010 and 2019 were linked to overlapping positive IOD (pIOD) and El Niños in 1997 and 2019, and with an El Niño alone in 2010. Combined pIOD and El Niño depressed sea level was the major factor in reducing cover in 1997 while El Niño extreme sea temperatures were responsible for large reductions in 2010. In 2019, a bi-phasic pIOD and El Niño resulted in lowered cover at a time of both decreased sea level and high sea temperature."
  - Sources: [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:33844871&resultType=core&format=json); [PubMed 33844871](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33844871/)
- Brown B.E. et al., a 17-year Ko Phuket reef-flat monitoring study (Marine Biology). Reef flats suffered "anomalously low sea levels in 1997–1998 as a result of climate-related events in the Indian Ocean", and seriation patterns broke down in 1998 "as a result of earlier negative sea-level anomalies". Inner reef flats were hit harder, consistent with emersion stress. — [Springer, doi:10.1007/s00227-002-0810-0](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00227-002-0810-0) (search snippet; not fetched in full)
- The 2010 Andaman mass bleaching (Phang Nga, Phuket, Krabi) was documented by DMCR's Marine and Coastal Resources Research and Development Institute. Per Dunne et al., it was driven by El Niño heat, not sea level. — [DMCR coral bleaching page](https://thailandcoralbleaching.dmcr.go.th/th/blog/13633); [DMCR warning/knowledge](https://warning.dmcr.go.th/th/knowledge/detail/1284) (search results; not fetched)
- Thai outreach on ENSO, e.g. NSTDA Sarawit "เอลนิญโญ-ลานิญญา วิกฤตภัยแล้งและอุทกภัย", frames ENSO's impact on Thailand mainly through rainfall, drought and floods. — [NSTDA](https://www.nstda.or.th/sci2pub/el-nino-la-nina/) (search snippet)
- Academic work on Gulf circulation describes the northeast monsoon (Nov–Jan) as controlling circulation in the upper Gulf. Thai review article: "การไหลเวียนของกระแสน้ำในอ่าวไทยตอนบน: การทบทวนงานวิจัย", Burapha Univ. Science Journal. — [BUU journal](https://journal.lib.buu.ac.th/index.php/science/article/view/10024) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- The mechanism chain the Thai and Phuket sources support for the Andaman runs: **positive IOD (often with El Niño) → easterly wind anomalies off Sumatra and Java → shoaling thermocline and lowered sea surface in the eastern Indian Ocean → Andaman sea level lowered by about 15–30 cm for months**. This is consistent with the Similan "cold water / thermocline near 50 m" report (Dec 2020).
- For the Gulf, the mechanisms sources describe are local and seasonal: monsoon wind setup (NE monsoon, Oct–Jan), spring tides, river runoff and weather systems. This is consistent with weak interannual (ENSO) coherence in our results.

### Gaps
- I found no Thai-language outreach article from Chulalongkorn, Kasetsart or PMBC that explicitly explains IOD → Andaman sea level in mechanistic terms. The DMCR Niphon Phongsuwan article is the closest, but the fetched text omits the mechanism.
- The full text of Dunne et al. (2021) was not read. Quantitative sea-level anomaly values in cm per event, and the tide-gauge station used, are therefore unconfirmed from that paper. DMCR's 30 cm (1997) and 15 cm (2019) figures are the best citable numbers found.
